01 / THE IDEA

One floating system.
Three connected jobs.

Water bodies can collect floating debris while their quality changes out of sight. AERON is designed to help teams observe conditions, remove visible surface waste, and make timely decisions using sensor data.

↗
01

Detect & collect

A camera-based detection concept identifies visible floating debris. A front scoop mesh catches waste while allowing water to drain back.

SURFACE WASTE
⌁
02

Measure water

Sensors monitor pH, turbidity, temperature, and dissolved oxygen to provide a clearer picture of changing water conditions.

Respond & report

When configured thresholds are crossed, AERON can raise an alert and trigger aeration logic. Readings are shown on a cloud-connected dashboard.

02 / SYSTEM WORKFLOW

From water surface
to useful insight.

01

Scan the surface

A camera captures surface images. An AI model, once trained and integrated, can classify visible objects such as bottles, wrappers, leaves, and foam.

VISION
02

Collect floating debris

The front scoop mesh collects and temporarily holds surface waste. It does not remove dissolved pollutants, and oil removal needs a separate specialized system.

COLLECTION
03

Monitor water quality

pH, turbidity, temperature, and dissolved-oxygen sensors send readings to the ESP32 controller for threshold checks and logging.

SENSING
04

Trigger an appropriate response

If dissolved oxygen falls below a configured threshold, the controller can switch on the aeration unit. Aeration supports oxygen transfer; it does not remove every type of pollution.

AERATION
05

Send data to the dashboard

The ESP32 transmits readings over Wi-Fi to a connected cloud service. The dashboard presents current readings, alerts, collection logs, and system status.

CLOUD
i

Prototype honesty matters. Predicting pollution 48–72 hours ahead requires enough historical, location-specific data and a validated forecasting model. Treat this as a development goal until it has been tested.
